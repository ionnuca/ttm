User guide

TTM keeps track of table tennis players, tournaments, matches and each player's rating. The home page shows the latest matches; the overall rankings are under Players.

1. Accounts and roles

RoleWhat it can do
Visitor (no account)Sees the matches, rankings, player pages and tournaments.
UserCreates an account with “Sign up” (every account is also a player, with an initial rating of 1000). Asks to register for tournaments (confirmed by an organiser), enters the results of their own matches, completes their profile and photo.
Tournament managerCreates and runs tournaments: registration, start, stage 2, finishing, correcting any result.
AdministratorEverything the manager does, plus users and roles, players (including the initial rating) and data backups.

2. How to run a tournament

  1. Creating. The manager (or administrator) presses “New tournament” under Tournaments and gives only the name and date. Registration opens immediately.
  2. Registration. Players with an account press “Sign up for it” on the tournament page: this sends a registration request, which waits for an organiser (administrator or tournament manager) to confirm it. Organisers get a notification and, on the tournament page under “Registration requests”, press Confirm or Reject. The player sees the answer in the notifications (the bell in the top bar). The organiser can also add or remove anyone directly; requests still unconfirmed are cancelled when the tournament starts.
  3. Starting. “Start tournament” closes registration. At that point you choose:
    • the type: Round robin or Groups + finals (with the number of groups);
    • the number of sets: best of 3, 5 or 7 (first to win 2, 3 or 4 sets);
    • optionally, a commercial tournament: the entry fee and the number of winners.
    The app forms the groups and generates all the matches, round by round.
  4. Results. Each match gets a set score (e.g. 3:1) or W – walkover, when the opponent does not play. Results are entered by the two players or by the organiser.
  5. Finishing. After the last result the tournament becomes “Finished” and the ratings are recalculated. The organiser can also finish it manually: matches without a result stay “unplayed” and count neither for the standings nor for the rating. Results can be corrected after the end as well; the rating is then recalculated automatically.

3. Tournament formats

Round robin – everybody plays everybody, in a single group ordered by rating.

Groups + finals – in two stages:

  1. Stage 1 – groups. The groups are formed by a random draw from rating pots, so they are balanced:
    • players are ordered by rating, highest first (equal ratings alphabetically);
    • the first players, as many as there are groups, form the first pot and are drawn at random, one into each group;
    • the procedure is repeated with the next players, pot by pot, until the list ends;
    • if the last pot is incomplete, its players also go at random into different groups (those groups have one player more).
    Example: 12 players in 3 groups. The pots are places 1–3, 4–6, 7–9 and 10–12 of the rankings. Each group gets one player from each pot, so the top three players are certainly in different groups, but who plays whom is decided by the draw. In the group table players are listed by rating. As long as no result has been recorded, the administrator can redo the draw with the “Redraw groups” button. Each group plays round robin.
  2. Stage 2 – finals. After the groups, the organiser chooses how many players from each group go to Final 1; the others play Final 2. Both finals are played round robin, but players who already met in the group do not play again: the stage 1 result is carried over. Once stage 2 has started, the group results can no longer be changed.

4. Points and group standings

ResultPoints
Win2
Loss (match played)1
Walkover loss (no-show)0

A walkover counts in sets as a clean-sheet win (3:0 in best of 5). In the group table each match is shown as a fraction: points on top, the set score below.

Places are decided by points. When points are equal, only the head-to-head matches between the tied players count, in order:

  1. points in those matches;
  2. the ratio of sets won / lost in those matches.

If a criterion separates only some of the players, the calculation restarts from step 1 for those still tied, using only the matches between them. Results against the other players in the group and the rating do not count.

If the tie cannot be broken:

  • regular tournament – the players share the place. The table marks it with “=”, e.g. three players in 1st place (1=, 1=, 1=), and the next one is 4th. In “Groups + finals”, if a shared place is among the qualifying places, all players on it go to Final 1;
  • commercial tournament – once all group matches are played, the players still tied play one tie-break set against each other, to 11 points (by 2 clear points), and the point score is recorded (e.g. 11:8). Again only among them, sets won decide, then the ratio of points won / lost. If the tie remains (rarely), a new round of sets is played. The tie-break sets are shown under the group table; the group (or tournament) ends only after they are played. They do not count for the rating or the statistics.

Example. Three players each win one match 3:0 (Ana – Ion 3:0, Ion – Dan 3:0, Dan – Ana 3:0). All have 3 points, and among themselves 3 sets won and 3 lost each. In a regular tournament all three are 1st; in a commercial one they play tie-break sets.

5. Commercial tournaments and prizes

The prize pool is the entry fee × the number of participants. It is split as follows:

WinnersSplit
1100%
260% / 40%
350% / 30% / 20%

Each prize is rounded to the nearest multiple of 50 lei (for example 420 → 400, 280 → 300, 225 → 250), so the prize total may differ slightly from the pool.

In Groups + finals, the winner of Final 2 receives the entry fee, and the rest of the pool is shared among the Final 1 winners according to the table above. Such a tournament can be finished manually only after stage 2 has started.

6. Rating (Elo)

Every player has a rating, which starts from the initial rating (1000 by default) and changes after each finished tournament. For each match played, the win probability is computed first, then the change:

EA = 1 / (1 + 10(RB − RA) / 400) ΔRA = K × (SA − EA)
  • RA, RB – the two players' ratings at the start of the tournament (all matches of a tournament use the same ratings, so the order of the matches does not matter);
  • SA – 1 for a win, 0 for a loss (the set score does not matter);
  • K – 40 while the player has fewer than 30 rated matches, then 20 (set at the start of each tournament);
  • a rating difference greater than 400 points counts as 400;
  • the changes from all matches of the tournament are added up and the total is rounded to a whole number;
  • walkovers do not change the rating (but they count for the group standings);
  • the rating cannot drop below 0.

Example. Ana (1500) beats Elena (1400), both with K = 40:

EAna = 1 / (1 + 10−100/400) = 0.64 ΔRAna = 40 × (1 − 0.64) = +14 EElena = 0.36 ΔRElena = 40 × (0 − 0.36) = −14

Had Elena won, the change would have been ±26: an unexpected win brings more points.

The rating is recalculated in chronological order, from the initial rating, over all finished tournaments. That is why a correction in an older tournament is also reflected in the tournaments after it.

7. Matches, players and profile

  • Matches (home page): the latest matches with a result, searchable by player or tournament. Next to the name is the rating change, once the tournament has finished.
  • Player page (press a name): the ranking position, statistics (matches, wins, percentage, sets, tournaments, best rating), equipment, rating history and all their matches.
  • My profile: personal details, playing hand, equipment, password, photo and language. The photo is cropped square for lists and can be viewed larger by pressing it.
  • Language: Romanian, Russian or English, from the RO / RU / EN switcher in the top bar. For users with an account the chosen language is saved and used every time they sign in.

8. The app on your phone

TTM can be used like a regular app. On iPhone: in Safari, the Share button → Add to Home Screen. On Android: in Chrome, the ⋮ menu → Add to Home screen.
